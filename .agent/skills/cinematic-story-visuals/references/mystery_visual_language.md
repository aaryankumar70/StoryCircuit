## 1. Identity
Curiosity and uncertainty. Moody but **readable**: the viewer should be able to inspect the evidence. Documentary-inspired, controlled contrast, subtle suspense. Unlike horror, the frame invites close reading rather than avoidance.

## 2. Palette
| Role | HEX | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Ink shadow | `#10151A` | Backgrounds and deep shadow |
| Slate | `#26323A` | Dominant base |
| Steel blue | `#4B6270` | Midtones, maps |
| Paper | `#CFC6AE` | Documents, photographs, highlights |
| Evidence amber | `#B8873B` | Single accent: the clue |
| Muted teal | `#3F6B68` | Secondary accent, subdued |
| Cold highlight | `#B9C4C6` | Brightest values |

Rule: **one clue colour** (amber) per frame; the audience learns to look there.

## 3. Evidence-first visual vocabulary
- Documents (letters, reports, ledgers), maps, photographs, clippings, objects on a surface
- Desks, boards, archives, case files, interiors with lived-in clutter
- Locations as clues (an exterior at the time of the event)
Legal and ethical care: when depicting real people, events or documents, use illustrative reconstruction (obscured text, silhouettes, symbolic objects), do not fabricate quotes, signatures or "evidence" that could be mistaken for the real artifact. Label reconstructed material as such in `implementation_notes`.

## 4. Composition and lighting
- **Top-down desk shots** with one pool of light, objects arranged in a readable triangle.
- **Pin-and-string / map shots** only when the script describes connections; no decorative conspiracy boards.
- **Documentary medium shots**, slight long-lens feel, mid-contrast (not crushed).
- **Practical lighting**: desk lamp, window, streetlight; neutral-to-warm key, cool fill.
- **Rack focus** from foreground object to background subject for a "notice this" beat.

## 5. Reveal structure
Plan reveals as three framings: **tease** (partial detail), **confirm** (full clear view), **consequence** (a reaction or wide that recontextualises). Each reveal needs a deliberate change: focus, light, crop or camera position.

## 6. Archival and documentary treatment
Grain, slight desaturation, soft vignette, subtle scan texture for archive-style inserts. Keep archive inserts visually distinct from present-day scenes (palette shift to warmer paper tones, slight contrast reduction). Do not overuse sepia.

## 7. Motion and pacing
- Slow pans along documents or maps to guide the eye (reading speed ~ 2-3 s per line of visible text; do not animate unreadable text).
- Push-ins toward the clue; pull-outs to show scale.
- Moderately faster cutting than horror (2.5-5 s) during discovery; hold on the key clue for 3-4 s.

## 8. Anti-patterns
- Unreadable gibberish text presented as evidence
- Cliche conspiracy walls and red string with no narrative basis
- Fabricated "classified" stamps, logos or real institutions' marks
- Overly dark frames in which evidence cannot be seen
- Stock detective imagery (trench coats, magnifying glasses) unless the script contains them

## 9. Scene recipes
**Discovery beat:** tease (macro of an edge) -> confirm (clear object) -> consequence (character silhouette). Three short scenes or one rack focus.
**Timeline beat:** map or document slow pan, one clue highlighted by amber light, no extra animation.
**Doubt beat:** shallow focus, a second object introduced in the background that contradicts the first.
