## 1. Identity
Psychological unease, not shock. The audience should feel observed, excluded or doomed by ordinary spaces. Reference the *language* of atmospheric psychological horror, analog horror and slow-burn mystery (patient framing, ordinary places made wrong, implication) without copying any specific scene, composition or protagonist from existing films.

## 2. Palette
| Role | HEX | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Deep shadow | `#0B0F12` | Darkest value; keep faint detail |
| Charcoal base | `#1B2429` | Dominant surface |
| Blue-grey mid | `#3A4A4F` | Walls, road, sky |
| Fog | `#6F7F84` | Depth and atmosphere |
| Bone highlight | `#A9B4B0` | Brightest value, never pure white |
| Muted green | `#5B6B58` | Foliage, vinyl, institutional paint |
| Dim tungsten | `#C9B27A` | Motivated practical lights only |

Rules: overall saturation 10-25%; **one** warm accent per frame; no red grading. Red may appear only as a small in-world object, once, justified by the script.

## 3. Lighting
- **Motivated sources only** (window, lamp, headlights, hallway strip).
- **Low-key** by default; faces partially lit, turned away or in silhouette.
- **Warm island in a cold frame**: a lamp is a small refuge that makes the surrounding dark feel larger.
- **Side and under-exposed fills**: let one side of a face fall off; never flatten it.
- **Light that fails**: slow dimming of a practical light is a high-value, low-cost tension tool.

## 4. Composition devices
- **Negative space**: leave 50-70% of the frame empty above/beside the subject; the emptiness is the threat.
- **Isolation**: a single small figure in a large space.
- **Doorway and window framing** (frame-within-frame) for inevitability.
- **Leading lines** (aisles, roads, hallways) that end in darkness.
- **Back-of-head / back-to-camera** to withhold faces and imply a shared knowledge.
- **Foreground obstruction** (a chair back, a door edge) to create the feeling of being watched.
- **Compression** with long-lens feel to stack seat rows or trees into a wall.

## 5. Environment and weather
Use fog, rain, wet surfaces, condensation, dust and still air only when the setting justifies them. Fog adds depth separation; rain adds sound and reflection; dust catches light shafts. Do not add all three to the same scene.

## 6. Texture and grain
Subtle analog grain, subtle vignette, slight halation on practical lights. Distortion is restrained: a one-frame softness shift, a brief chroma separation on a revelation. No sustained glitch, VHS overlays or screen shake.

## 7. Motion and pacing
- Default: static or barely perceptible push-in (scale delta <= 0.06 across a scene).
- Stillness during the worst moments; motion during realisation.
- Cuts are clean. Dissolves are for time passing. Dip-to-black is for endings and breaths.
- See `transitions_and_motion.md`.

## 8. Dread techniques (without monsters)
1. **The empty seat / empty chair**: absence as presence.
2. **The partly open door** with a vertical strip of light.
3. **The listener's silhouette**: someone who knows something, seen from behind.
4. **Hands**: extreme close-ups carry tension without faces.
5. **Delay**: hold a frame two seconds longer than comfortable.
6. **Scale**: shrink the subject within a vast, ordinary space.
7. **Withheld content**: never visualise the forbidden story, name or event; show reactions.
8. **Repetition with variation**: a motif returns slightly changed (closer, darker, emptier).

## 9. Show / hide decisions
Always write `hidden_or_withheld` for key scenes. Reveal rule: **the reveal is a framing change, not an object.** A reveal can be a rack focus, a light turning on, a character turning around or a door opening, never a creature appearing.

## 10. Anti-patterns (fail conditions)
- Gore, excessive blood, cartoonish or "spooky" imagery
- Jump-scare zooms or flash frames
- Random red filters
- Glowing eyes
- Generic haunted-house exteriors, cobwebs, ouija boards
- Constant camera shake
- Bright saturated colours
- Unnecessary monsters or supernatural entities that the script does not contain

## 11. Scene recipes
**Dread beat (narration states something terrible quietly):** static wide or ECU on hands; low-key; no atmosphere motion except dust; hold 5-7 s.
**Realisation beat (narrator understands):** slow push-in 1.00 -> 1.06; one light change; cut on the key word.
**Memory beat:** slightly softer contrast (-10%), no atmosphere layer except dust, static or push-in, childhood states only.
**Threshold beat (approaching the unavoidable):** doorway framing, narrator back-to-camera, light strip, micro-drift only in the final sequence.
**Ending beat (preserve inevitability):** do not resolve the frame; end on a held composition and dip to black; never show the consequence.
