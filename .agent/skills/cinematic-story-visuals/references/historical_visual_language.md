## 1. Identity
Period-appropriate environments, grounded clothing, architecture and objects; documentary-inspired cinematic compositions; atmospheric environmental shots. Beauty is allowed; invention for beauty's sake is not.

## 2. Accuracy first
**Never introduce historically inaccurate objects, clothing or architecture to look attractive.** When uncertain, show less: a silhouette, a distant view, a detail shot of a safe object, or an abstracted environment. Precision beats spectacle.

## 3. Period research checklist
Before writing a bible, record for the scene's place and year:
- Architecture and building materials; street surface and lighting source (candle, oil, gas, electric)
- Clothing by class and occupation; hats, footwear, fabrics
- Transport and tools; weapons and military equipment only if stated
- Signage, language and writing style; money and typical objects
- Typical weather and light for the setting
- What did **not** exist yet (check: electric light, tarmac, plastic, zips, modern glass, brand logos)
Record unknowns in `implementation_notes` as assumptions. Prefer supplied images from verified archives when the pipeline has them.

| 18th-19th c. (oil/gas) | `#1D2024` | `#43494B` | `#C8B98E` | `#7A5C2E` |
| Early 20th c. (early electric) | `#1B1F22` | `#4A5357` | `#CFC8B2` | `#A07A3A` |

Light source must match the period (do not use LED-style white light in a candle era).

## 5. Composition and lighting
- Wide establishing shots with architectural layering (foreground arch, midground figures, background skyline).
- Medium shots of workers, crowds, craftspeople; hands and tools as detail inserts.
- Candle, oil lamp, window light as practical sources; soft, warm-to-neutral, low saturation.
- Atmospheric: smoke, dust, mist, rain on cobbles, seasonally accurate.

## 6. Documentary techniques
- Slow pans across maps, plans, engravings; pushes into period objects.
- Archive-style treatments (grain, tone shifts) only for explicit archival inserts, clearly separate from re-created scenes.
- Silhouettes at distance for large historical figures when portrait accuracy is uncertain.

## 7. Handling uncertainty
Hierarchy of fallback: (1) verified supplied image, (2) accurate reconstruction of environment, (3) detail insert of a safe object, (4) silhouette, (5) abstract atmospheric shot (sky, water, ground). Do not invent specific faces for real historical figures; keep them silhouetted, partial or distant.

## 8. Anti-patterns
- Anachronisms (modern buildings, wires, plastic, zips, modern glass, clean uniforms in muddy settings)
- Fantasy armour, Hollywood-style costume cliches when the script is factual
- Glamorising violence; gore
- "Epic" teal-orange grading regardless of era
- Wrong cultural attributes (mixing regions' architecture)

## 9. Scene recipes
**Establishing beat:** wide, foreground arch or doorway, mid figures, low horizon, fog or smoke, slow push-in.
**Event beat:** medium crowd silhouette, one practical light, slow pan following the action.
**Aftermath beat:** wide static, emptiness, a single object in focus.
